# Transport SaaS

## Product

This is a multi-tenant SaaS platform for small and medium staff transportation companies.

The product is expected to cover organizations, customers, vehicles, drivers, routes, shifts, daily operations, expenses, revenue and payments, and reports.

Workflows and validation for those areas are not specified yet. Do not invent roles or a permission matrix.

## Stack

The initial stack is Angular, TypeScript, Nx, Supabase, PostgreSQL, Vercel, and Cloudflare.

A later version may move the API to NestJS and host PostgreSQL independently. Add Redis, background workers, or object storage only when a feature needs them.

## Workspace

The Angular application is `apps/routeops`, tagged `type:app`. Browser checks are `apps/routeops-e2e`, tagged `type:e2e`. `libs/site` is tagged `scope:site` and `type:feature`; it holds the public pages: the home page at `/`, which sends a signed-in visitor to `/dashboard` and links the best offer running in the visitor's date to the pricing page, the pricing page at `/pricing`, which reads the plans on offer from `subscription_plans`, the discounts from `subscription_periods`, and the public offers from `subscription_offers`, shows each offer running on a shown plan, lets a visitor choose a period and type a fleet size to see the plan that takes it, shows each plan's price after the period discount and then its largest offer with the full price struck through, and sends every request to the contact page, and the contact page at `/contact`, which links the phone, WhatsApp, and email kept in `domain/contact-details.ts` and stores nothing. `libs/organizations` is tagged `scope:organizations` and `type:feature`; it holds the sign-in page and the signed-in dashboard at `/dashboard`, which shows today's trips as scheduled, done, not done yet, and cancelled with how much of the day is done, the month's done trips, revenue, expenses, and net profit through the same `trip_report` and `expense_totals` as the reports with what share of the revenue the expenses took, and what still needs recording: past working days nobody opened, the past month's pay that `driver_pay` and `vehicle_pay` show still unrecorded, and done trips with no price, each linking to the page and day or month that fixes it, and links to every section; until the organization has a route, it shows the setup steps instead: customers, drivers, then routes. `libs/customers` is tagged `scope:customers` and `type:feature`. `libs/vehicles` is tagged `scope:vehicles` and `type:feature`; a vehicle is `owned` by the company, `rented`, or a `contractor`'s own vehicle, and a rented or contractor vehicle names its owner, with an optional phone, and may carry a `rent_type` of `monthly` with a `monthly_rent`, or `per_trip` with an `outbound_rent` and a `return_rent`; a company-owned vehicle has no rent. A company with a subscription may not have more active vehicles than its limit: the `vehicles` triggers raise `vehicle_limit` when a vehicle is added active or made active again beyond the limit of the latest subscription that has started, even one that has ended, and a company with no subscription has no limit. Each vehicle has a page at `/vehicles/:vehicleId` that shows a chosen month in the same way: the vehicle's trips from `trip_log`, the done and extra counts, their revenue, the expenses naming the vehicle by category through `expense_totals`, its share of its routes' price when they split it, the profit as the reports' vehicle view counts it, its share less its expenses when it earns one, the rent or contractor pay from `vehicle_pay` with what is still unrecorded, the routes it runs now, and the unopened days. `libs/drivers` is tagged `scope:drivers` and `type:feature`; a driver's `pay_type` is `none`, `salary` for an employee, or `per_trip`; an employee has a `monthly_salary` and may name `salary_trips`, the outbound trips and as many return trips the salary covers each month, and then an `outbound_pay` and a `return_pay`, which may differ; an employee without `salary_trips` has a fixed salary and may still have both amounts, and then each extra trip the driver drives, one a member added to a day by hand, earns its direction's amount on top of the salary, with nothing taken off for absence; a driver paid per trip has only those two amounts. Triggers keep a driver's pay terms per month in `driver_pay_terms` and a vehicle's rent terms in `vehicle_rent_terms`, which members only read: a change applies from the organization's month it is made in, so a past month keeps its terms, and a month before the first saved terms uses those first terms. A customer, driver, or vehicle that an active route uses cannot be made inactive; the database raises `active_routes` with the route names, and an active route cannot name an inactive one (`inactive_choice`). A driver may have one photo: the browser crops it to a 256-pixel square and re-encodes it as WebP, or JPEG where WebP is unavailable, which also drops its location data, and stores it under `<organization_id>/<driver_id>/` in the private `driver-photos` storage bucket, which accepts only those two types up to 100 KB; `drivers.photo_path` names the file, each new photo is a new file and the old one is deleted, the list shows it through short-lived signed links, and storage row level security limits it to members of an active organization. The drivers list marks a license that expired, or that expires within the same 30 days the notifications use. Each driver has a page at `/drivers/:driverId` that shows a chosen month, the current one by default: the trips `trip_log` returns for the driver, both those the driver drove and those planned for the driver that another driver took, each with its status under the `trip_report` done rule, the done, extra, and absent counts, the revenue of the done trips the driver drove, the month's pay from `driver_pay` with what is still unrecorded, the routes the driver runs now, and the unopened days. `libs/routes` is tagged `scope:routes` and `type:feature`; a route links a customer, a vehicle, and a driver of the same organization, and composite foreign keys on `organization_id` enforce that in the database. A route belongs to one customer; the same route name may serve several customers but is unique within one customer. Every route and every daily trip, extra trips included, names its driver; the vehicle may still be set later. A route may carry a `trip_price`, what its customer pays for one day of it: a route with an outbound and a return time is a full trip, a shift, priced as one, and a route that runs one direction is priced for that trip. `route_trip_share` gives each direction its share, half a shift's price in whole minor units with the outbound trip taking the odd one, or the whole price for a one-direction route, so a day where only one direction of a shift ran earns half; `prepare_daily_trips` copies that share onto each planned trip and keeps a coming trip in step with its route, while a past or done trip keeps its price. A route whose vehicle is company-owned may split its shift price: an optional management margin, a percent of the price or a fixed amount per shift, is taken first, and the vehicle's share comes from what is left, as a percent of it, a fixed amount per shift, or all of it; what remains is for the driver and the company, whose split is not decided yet. `trip_split` gives each trip its part, the triggers copy it onto each route trip as `vehicle_share` and `management_share` and keep coming trips in step, a past trip keeps its shares and one saved without them counts at its route's split, and shares count only while the trip's vehicle is company-owned; a rented or contractor vehicle keeps its rent instead. `libs/operations` is tagged `scope:operations` and `type:feature`; it keeps one daily trip per route, day, and direction, copied from the routes by `prepare_daily_trips`. A past day opened late gets only the routes that existed on it, an opened past day never changes on its own, and a route change updates its trips on coming days already opened. For trips that ran before their routes were set up, a member may add chosen routes, inactive ones included, to past days, up to 93 at a time, through `add_route_trips`: it opens each day first, adds each route's trips on its working days with the route's current driver, vehicle, and price share, leaves trips already there alone, and returns how many it added. Each trip keeps `planned_driver_id`, the driver its route named when it was planned, so an absence counts against that driver even after the route changes driver. A member may also add an extra trip to a day; it belongs to a customer and optionally one of that customer's routes, may carry its own price, `prepare_daily_trips` never changes it, and a member may delete any trip of the day, including a route trip marked done: deleting a route trip records an omission so `prepare_daily_trips` does not put it back, the daily screen lists those omissions with a restore action through `restore_omitted_trip` for the same day including today, and `add_route_trips` also clears omissions when the route is added again to past days. Each trip occupies its driver and vehicle from departure until arrival. A driver or vehicle may run more than one shift or trip in a day, including on different vehicles for the same driver, but two non-cancelled trips of the same driver or the same vehicle must not overlap on that day; ending when another starts is allowed, and the database and the daily and route screens refuse an overlap. Each organization chooses in `operations_settings` how trips are recorded: automatically, where a trip counts as done once its day comes unless it is cancelled, or manually, where a trip counts as done only after a member marks it done; a trip marked done always counts as done. A change applies from the organization's today onward: `set_trip_recording` starts a period in `trip_recording_periods`, and `trip_recording_on` gives the way in force on a day, so past days keep how they were recorded. A cancellation or change carries a reason, and putting a cancelled trip back to running without changing its driver or vehicle clears that reason; a trip nobody changed or marked done follows its route until its day has passed. `trip_log` lists a period's trips for one driver, matching the driver or the planned driver, or for one vehicle, each with its status, `done` under the `trip_report` rule, `unrecorded` when manual recording still waits for it, `planned`, or `cancelled`, and its price or its share of its route's. `libs/reports` is tagged `scope:reports` and `type:feature`; it shows a month's done trips per customer, vehicle, and driver, counted in the database by `trip_report` under the same done rule, with their revenue at each trip's price, or its share of its route's price when the trip has none, and how many done trips have no price at all. A member may open a contracting-company invoice for a chosen date range, listing each route with its vehicle type, full trip count, unit rate when every trip shared one price, and revenue, and may download it as Excel (CSV) or print it as PDF. Screens count trips as full trips: `trip_halves` makes one direction of a route with both times half a trip and any other trip a whole one, `trip_report` adds them up as `done_halves` and `extra_halves` and `trip_log` gives each trip's `halves`, so the reports, the dashboard, and the driver and vehicle pages show a day where only one direction ran as half, while pay keeps counting each direction. It warns with `unopened_days` about working days nobody opened, because a day's trips exist only once it was opened. It also subtracts the month's expenses, totalled by `expense_totals`, to show net profit, and the vehicle view shows each vehicle's expenses and profit, with expenses that name no vehicle on their own line. The route view shows each route's revenue less a driver's share and a vehicle's share, and what remains is the company's share: an expense naming a driver is the driver's when it is a salary or names no vehicle, any other expense naming a vehicle is the vehicle's, and each driver's and vehicle's recorded expenses are split over the routes by the done trips on each; a vehicle that earns a share of its routes' price puts that share on each route instead and pays its own expenses from it, the management margin has its own column, and the company-owned vehicles' net is shown on its own line, so net profit stays the revenue less every expense; expenses no route carries are shown apart, and the view warns, through `driver_pay` and `vehicle_pay`, about pay due with nothing recorded. `libs/expenses` is tagged `scope:expenses` and `type:feature`; an expense has a day, one category of `fuel`, `maintenance`, `salaries`, `rent`, `contractors`, `licenses`, `tolls`, `office`, or `other`, an amount above zero, an optional vehicle and driver of the same organization, and a description that `other` requires. For each driver with pay terms, `driver_pay` returns the month's done outbound and return trips the driver actually drove under the `trip_report` done rule, so a substitute earns the trip, the trips planned for the driver that were cancelled or handed to another driver with the reason `driver_absent`, and the salary already recorded, all under the pay terms of that month. The screen suggests, per direction, a per-trip driver's amount times the done trips, or an employee's salary plus the amount for each done trip beyond `salary_trips`, less the amount for each trip below it that was missed through absence, so absences first use up extra trips and no other reason costs the driver pay; without `salary_trips` the salary is fixed. The member may change that amount before recording it, but not its month, category, driver, or vehicle, and pay counts as an expense only once a member records it, so it is never counted twice; pay still due is the suggested amount less what is recorded. When a cost is split, the shares are whole minor units that add up to the cost. In the same way, `vehicle_pay` returns each vehicle with rent terms, its done outbound and return trips, and the `rent` or `contractors` expenses already recorded for it; the screen suggests the monthly rent or the trips times their amounts, and recording it adds a `rent` expense for a rented vehicle or a `contractors` expense for a contractor's. `libs/notifications` is tagged `scope:notifications` and `type:feature`; it is the bell in the app shell's top bar, which the shell loads only once the browser is idle and only while an organization is chosen. It stores nothing and adds no database objects: it reads what needs a member from the data that already exists, `trip_log`, `unopened_days`, and the past month's `driver_pay` and `vehicle_pay` under the shared pay rule, and lists, the urgent first, driver and vehicle licenses that expired or expire within 30 days, past working days nobody opened, today's trips of the same vehicle or driver less than an hour apart, trips still waiting under manual recording once their departure time has passed, the past month's unrecorded driver and vehicle pay, and done trips with no price, each linking to the page and day or month that fixes it. ## Assumptions

- The tenant boundary is an organization. A user may belong to more than one organization through a membership. The active organization is one of those memberships.
- Until an application backend exists, PostgreSQL row level security enforces that boundary.
- Role names and permissions are not decided.
- Money is stored as a `bigint` count of the currency's minor unit, such as piasters, and amounts are typed with at most that many decimals, so nothing is rounded. Each organization works in one currency, `organizations.currency`, chosen by the operator at provisioning; Egyptian pounds are the default, and members cannot change it.
- Each organization has a `time_zone`, Cairo by default, chosen by the operator at provisioning from a fixed list. "Today" for a company is its local date: the database uses `organization_today` and `organization_date`, never the server's UTC date. Do not add a money library.
- The Nx workspace will have one Angular application under `apps/` and libraries under `libs/`. Do not add a NestJS application in the initial setup.
- The site operator provisions each company. There is no public self-signup. The operator creates the organization and one email-and-password login, and that login is a membership of that organization. Creating those credentials happens on a server-side path, because the service-role key must stay out of the Angular build.
- The operator is the person who provisions tenants. That is not an in-company permission role. Do not invent roles inside a customer organization.
- The interface is in Arabic and English, for Egypt and other Arab countries. Arabic is the default and is written right to left; English is written left to right. The app shell has a sidebar with the active organization's sections and a top bar whose button switches language without a reload, and the browser remembers the choice. `libs/shared/i18n` holds the current language and `injectText`. Each feature keeps its own `ui/<feature>-text.ts` with an `ar` and an `en` entry of the same shape. Domain rules and errors return codes, never sentences; the `ui` layer turns a code into text. Server functions also return codes. Use Modern Standard Arabic for Arabic screen text and user-facing messages. Write screen text so a transport company understands it without help: plain words, a short hint under each section and unclear field, and optional fields marked "(اختياري)". In the interface, customers are "الشركات المتعاقدة" (the companies whose staff the transport company carries). Code, identifiers, and database names stay English.
- In-app payment collection is a later feature library. Until that feature starts, a company pays the operator outside the product. The operator stops a company's account, `organizations.is_active`, when its monthly subscription is unpaid and turns it back on once it pays; a stopped company keeps its data, and a restrictive row level security policy "only active organizations" on every tenant table keeps its members from reading or changing it, so every new tenant table needs that policy too. Do not add a payment provider, checkout, or payment columns on organization or operations tables. The operator records each subscription a company pays by hand in `subscription_payments`, in the company's currency; only `platform_operators` read, add, or delete those rows, a company never sees them, and the table has no "only active organizations" policy so the operator still sees what a stopped company paid. The operator sells plans by the number of active vehicles: `subscription_plans` holds each plan's currency, Arabic and English names, vehicle limit, none meaning no limit, and monthly price, none meaning agreed per company, and anyone may read a plan on offer; `subscription_periods` holds the discount for paying 1, 3, 6, or 12 months at once, one month always at full price. Only `platform_operators` add or change plans and discounts. The operator records what each company bought in `organization_subscriptions`: the plan in the company's currency, its months, its start, the day its end is set by a trigger, the vehicle limit and the price agreed for the whole period, both suggested from the plan and changeable; only `platform_operators` read, add, correct, or delete those rows, a correction never changes the company, and the trigger sets the end again, and like the payments they have no "only active organizations" policy. To win companies the operator runs offers in `subscription_offers`: a `percent` off chosen plans and periods, taken off after the period's discount, or a free `trial` of chosen plans for `trial_days` days, each from a first day to an optional last day; an offer without a `code` is public, and anyone reads it while it is active, while one with a code is read only by `platform_operators` and applied when a company gives the code; only `platform_operators` add or change offers, and offers are stopped, never deleted. The operator chooses the offer for each company when recording a subscription, which keeps it in `offer_id`; the trigger refuses an offer that does not take the plan or, for a percent, the months (`offer_mismatch`), and makes a trial a subscription of the offer's days with no months and a price of zero, which a company gets only before any other subscription (`trial_used`). `operator_vehicle_usage` gives the operator each company's active vehicles and today, and the operator's screen lists each company's plan, period, days left, and active vehicles against its limit. When a company login forgets its password, the operator sets a new one through the `set-company-password` server function, which changes only logins that belong to a company, never an operator. Trip revenue is recorded from route and extra-trip prices and expenses are entered in `libs/expenses`; customer payments are not recorded yet.

## Workflow

Before a significant feature:

1. Inspect the existing architecture.
2. Identify the affected features and layers.
3. Explain the approach, including any database, security, and test impact.
4. State assumptions when a business rule is still open.
5. Implement the smallest maintainable change.
6. Run the workspace lint, type-check, and tests.
7. Report what changed and any remaining risk.

The agent writes code only. It does not commit, push, or write commit messages; the user reviews, commits, and pushes.

Add a library only when the feature needs it, and say why. Leave unrelated code alone.

## Definition of done

A feature is complete when:

- TypeScript types check and lint passes.
- Tests for the business behavior in the change pass.
- Any schema change is a migration committed to Git.
- Tenant isolation for the change is enforced in the database.
- Data screens cover loading, success, empty, and error.
- No secrets are committed.
- Architecture notes are updated when the module structure changes.
